What we do

Five things we help with

Most engagements start with one of these and grow into the others. You can also take any of them on their own.

  • 01

    AI tools, chosen and set up properly

    There are hundreds of AI tools and most of them won't help you. We start from the work you actually do, shortlist the two or three that fit, and get them running — accounts, access, settings and the guardrails that keep your data where it should be.

    • Honest tool comparison, no reseller incentives
    • Pilot design, rollout and adoption support
    • Data handling, access rules and acceptable-use policy
    • Fitting tools to the software you already run
  • 02

    Understanding where your productivity goes

    Before anything changes, we map how work moves through your team and find the parts that are slow, repetitive or done twice. You get a clear picture of where the time goes and an honest view of which bits AI can help with — and which bits it can't.

    • Workflow and time mapping with the people doing the work
    • Task-by-task review: automate, assist, or leave alone
    • Baseline measurement, then measure again after
    • A prioritised plan ranked by effort and payoff
  • 03

    Coding and debugging know-how

    You don't need a computer science degree to automate a spreadsheet or make sense of an error message. We teach the practical core: reading code, writing small scripts, getting useful code out of an AI assistant, and working out why something broke.

    • Reading and editing code without fear
    • Scripting basics — Python, spreadsheets, the command line
    • Prompting for code that runs, and checking that it does
    • Debugging: finding the real problem, not the first one
    • Version control explained without the jargon
  • 04

    Seminars and workshops

    Hands-on sessions built around your work rather than generic slides. An hour to two days, in person or online, for anything from a team of six to a full department — and people leave having actually done something.

    • Executive briefings: what to expect, what to fund, what to avoid
    • Team workshops on your own documents and data
    • Department-wide seminars and lunch-and-learns
    • Bespoke sessions written for your sector
  • 05

    Skill development programmes

    A structured route from "I've heard of ChatGPT" to people who build and maintain their own tools. Cohorts run over several weeks, with practice between sessions and someone to ask when you get stuck.

    • Three levels — Foundation, Practitioner, Advanced
    • Cohort-based or one-to-one
    • Project-based, using your real work as the material
    • Progress checks, a final project and a completion certificate
  • 06

    Not sure which you need?

    Most people aren't, and that's fine. A short call is usually enough to tell whether the answer is a two-hour workshop, a six-week programme, or something much simpler that doesn't need us at all.

    • 30 minutes, free, no slide deck
    • We'll say so if AI isn't the answer

How we work

Four steps, and then we leave

The aim is a team that doesn't need us on retainer. Every engagement is built to hand over.

  1. STEP 01

    Discovery

    A free 30-minute call. You describe the work and what's frustrating about it; we tell you honestly whether we can help and roughly what it would take.

  2. STEP 02

    Assessment

    We sit with the people doing the work, map how tasks actually flow, and write it up: where the time goes, what could change, what it's worth and what it would cost.

  3. STEP 03

    Build and train

    We set the tools up, run the workshops, and work through real tasks with your team — not demos. People learn on the things they'll be doing again next week.

  4. STEP 04

    Handover

    Written documentation, internal champions who know how it works, and a review at ninety days to see what stuck and what didn't.

Questions

The ones we get asked most

If yours isn't here, ask it on the call — we'd rather answer it before you commit to anything.

Do we need technical staff to get anything out of this?

No. Most of the people we train have never written a line of code, and the Foundation level assumes nothing at all. The coding and debugging material exists precisely so that non-technical teams stop being blocked waiting for someone else.

Which AI tools do you recommend?

It depends on the work and on the rules you're bound by. We don't resell anything and we take no commission, so the shortlist we give you is based on fit — including the option of carrying on without any new tool, which is sometimes the right answer.

What about our data and confidentiality?

We treat it as the first question rather than the last. Part of the assessment is establishing what can leave your systems and what can't, which tools honour that, and what your staff should be told in writing. Where sensitive material is involved we'll steer you towards arrangements that keep it in place.

How long does a typical engagement take?

A workshop is a day. An assessment is usually two to three weeks including the write-up. A full skill-development programme runs four to eight weeks depending on level. Plenty of clients start with a single workshop and decide from there.

Can you run sessions remotely?

Yes — the whole programme works online, and mixed cohorts across several sites are common. Some things land better in a room, so we'll say when we think travelling is worth it.

What does it cost?

It depends on scope, group size and whether we're travelling. We quote a fixed price per piece of work rather than a day rate, so you know the number before anything starts. The discovery call is free and carries no obligation.

What if AI isn't the right answer for us?

Then we'll tell you. A fair share of the problems we're called in for turn out to be a spreadsheet that needs restructuring or a process with a step nobody needs. Saying so is cheaper for you and better for us than selling a programme that won't help.
